# Team saturation judgment and coder agreement

Saturation and agreement are human analytic judgments supported by, not
replaced by, descriptive statistics.

## Saturation curve

Track new-code accumulation across analytic rounds:

1. Order sources in the analytic sequence chosen by the team.
2. For each round, count codes observed for the first time.
3. Plot the cumulative code count and the new-code rate (new codes / codes
   observed in the round).

A new-code rate below a pre-specified threshold (e.g. 0.05) is a **descriptive
signal**, not proof of saturation. Saturation also depends on:

- Information power and the breadth of the research question.
- Sampling strategy and the diversity of cases.
- Negative-case analysis and code granularity.
- Reflexivity and the research team's interpretive depth.

Never declare saturation from a curve alone.

## Coder agreement

- **Cohen's kappa** — two coders, nominal codes, chance-corrected agreement.
- **Krippendorff's alpha** — generalizes to multiple coders, missing values,
  and four metric levels: nominal, ordinal, interval, ratio.

Interpretation is context-dependent; a high alpha does not establish validity
or the quality of interpretation. Report the metric level, the coincidence
matrix, and the observed/expected disagreement.

## When to compute agreement

- After codebook training and before full coding (calibration).
- On a subset of the data during coding (monitoring).
- Agreement is a reliability check, not a substitute for adjudication of
  divergent cases.

## Boundaries

- Zero new codes does not establish saturation.
- Alpha measures agreement under its metric assumptions; it does not validate
  coding or interpretation.
- Assess saturation and agreement together with the research team, not from the
  script output alone.
