# Review templates and reporting-guideline checklists

A review skeleton structures your reading; a reporting-guideline checklist
audits completeness. Neither replaces actual expert review.

## Venue-specific review templates

Choose the template that matches the venue:

- **Journal** — summary, significance, strengths, major/minor issues,
  reproducibility, recommendation.
- **Conference** — summary, clarity, technical correctness, novelty,
  evaluation, questions, score.
- **Preprint** — summary, core claims, methods/transparency, suggestions.
- **Grant** — significance, innovation, investigator/environment, approach,
  impact.

Each section carries a prompt to guide the reviewer. Fill every section with
evidence tied to a manuscript location; do not fabricate comments.

## Reporting-guideline checklists

Score the manuscript against the appropriate guideline:

- **CONSORT** — randomized controlled trials (20+ items: title, abstract,
  design, randomization, blinding, flow, outcomes, harms, registration,
  funding).
- **STROBE** — observational studies (cohort, cross-sectional, case-control;
  ~18 items covering design, setting, variables, bias, results).
- **PRISMA** — systematic reviews and meta-analyses (~17 items: question,
  sources, search, selection, risk of bias, synthesis, heterogeneity,
  funding).

## Compliance scoring

Each item is scored by keyword presence in the manuscript text:

- **present** — a keyword group for the item is found.
- **absent** — no keyword group matches.

The compliance score is the fraction of applicable items addressed. This is a
descriptive screen only; it does not verify the quality, correctness, or
completeness of reporting.

## Using the checklist

1. Select the guideline matching the study design.
2. Provide the manuscript text and the list of sections present.
3. Review the absent items and confirm whether they are genuinely missing or
   merely use different terminology.
4. Attach the checklist findings to the review template as supporting evidence.

## Boundances

- A high compliance score does not replace expert review.
- Keyword scoring can miss nuance and can be fooled by boilerplate.
- Never claim an experiment, analysis, citation check, or manuscript change
  was completed unless an artifact or location verifies it.
